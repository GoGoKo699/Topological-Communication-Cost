# Communication bounds from the local overlap obstruction

The central step is to keep the probability of each communication branch. A normalized postselected state need not be preparable by a local trace-preserving circuit. Its subnormalized contribution can nevertheless be dominated by the output of such a circuit after every communicated bit is fixed.

We use the [adaptive model](model.md): each branch has local depth at most $D$; the seed and previous transcript fix all support partitions, senders, recipients, and clock positions; private outcomes remain local until transmitted.

## 1. The no-message input to the proof

For the cell torus with $N=2L^2$ and gate range $r\ge1$, define

$$
m_{L,r}(D)
=\left\lfloor\frac{L}{32(rD+1)}\right\rfloor^2,
\qquad
q_{L,r}(D)
=\left(\frac{16}{17}\right)^{m_{L,r}(D)}.
$$

The [channel-overlap theorem](channel-overlap.md) proves that every no-message local CPTP protocol in the model obeys

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{TC}}\rho)\le q_{L,r}(D).
$$

This includes arbitrary finite local ancillas, private records, resets, and discards. It holds after conditioning on every value of the public seed. When the floor is zero, $q_{L,r}(D)=1$ and this particular estimate is trivial.

For brevity write $m=m_{L,r}(D)$, $q=q_{L,r}(D)$, and

$$
\lambda=\log_2(17/16).
$$

## 2. Fixed-transcript domination

**Lemma.** Fix a public seed value $s$ and a complete finite transcript $t$. Let $\sigma_{t\mid s}$ be the subnormalized data output of that branch, so its trace is the branch probability. There is a no-message local CPTP circuit with output $\widehat\rho_{t\mid s}$, with the same local-depth cap and spatial range, such that

$$
0\le \sigma_{t\mid s}\le \widehat\rho_{t\mid s}.
$$

### Construction of the completed circuit

Preprogram every remotely supplied control value to its value in $t$. At every prefix of $t$, the model supplies a fixed local support pattern and sender. Thus all global message-dependent controls become known constants, and every remaining operation is local.

Keep all private records. A message generated by a stored classical bit is selected by a local CP trace-nonincreasing map at its sender. More general functions of local classical records have the same representation after onsite computation. The actual branch map includes only the selected outcome. Averaging over private records that were never communicated is already included in the local channels; those records are not fixed or counted as additional transcript bits.

Concretely, let $Q_b$ project onto the sender's classical record values that produce bit $b$. The selection and its completion are

$$
\mathcal M_b(X)=Q_bXQ_b,\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal M}(X)=Q_0XQ_0+Q_1XQ_1.
$$

The difference $\widehat{\mathcal M}-\mathcal M_b$ is CP. Because the record is classical, the joint state is block diagonal in its record basis, even when the record is correlated with quantum data. Thus $\widehat{\mathcal M}$ acts as the identity on every valid intermediate state. The completed physical protocol simply omits the consistency check. It adds no quantum operation or local layer.

For completeness, a selected CP trace-nonincreasing instrument map $\mathcal M$ inside a counted local layer also has a local CPTP completion. Let $Q=I-\mathcal M^\dagger(I)\ge0$. Choose a normalized state $\tau$ of its local output space that is block diagonal on every designated classical output register; a fixed classical basis state tensored with any normalized quantum state suffices. Then

$$
\mathcal R(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(QX)\tau,
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal M}=\mathcal M+\mathcal R
$$

has the required properties. The measure-and-prepare map $\mathcal R$ stays in that same counted support and layer, and its output remains a valid classical–quantum state. Consequently, later classical-read completions still act as the identity on valid states. We do not use this observation to grant a fresh uncounted quantum instrument at a message event.

Later operations use the fixed transcript $t$, including on the added completion branches. Their private local controls are defined on every local record value, as required by the model. Any extra failure label can be retained locally. Neither consistency checking nor failure reporting is sent to another cell.

The completed circuit therefore remains local and trace preserving. It contains the actual consistent branch as a positive contribution. Its support schedule is exactly the one specified by $t$, even if records on some added branches would have led to a different transcript in the original protocol. It is not required to reproduce the original protocol on those inconsistent histories.

### CP-order proof

Write the fixed branch as a finite composition

$$
\Phi_n\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1,
$$

including local channels and the selected sender maps. Let $\widehat\Phi_i=\Phi_i$ for trace-preserving steps and use the completions above for selected steps. The difference between the completed and selected compositions is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\widehat\Phi_n\circ\cdots\circ\widehat\Phi_1
-\Phi_n\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1\\
&\quad=
\sum_{j=1}^n
\widehat\Phi_n\circ\cdots\circ\widehat\Phi_{j+1}
\circ(\widehat\Phi_j-\Phi_j)
\circ\Phi_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Every summand is completely positive. Applying this identity to the initial state and tracing out private registers proves the operator inequality in the lemma. The intermediate maps can act on different register spaces; the composition identity is unchanged when their input and output spaces are tracked.

The classical-read completions can be omitted on valid states, and instrument completions stay in their existing local layers. Their dilations therefore obey the same radius $rD$ used by the channel-overlap theorem.

It follows immediately that

$$
a_{t\mid s}
:=\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{TC}}\sigma_{t\mid s})
\le q.
$$

The preprogrammed constants may include bits that occur late in $t$. This is an auxiliary local circuit proving an inequality for one fixed branch, not a method for predicting messages during an actual execution.

## 3. Worst-case communication

**Theorem.** If every complete transcript has length at most the integer $C$, then

$$
F\le \min\{1,\,2^Cq_{L,r}(D)\}.
$$

For each seed, a binary tree of maximum leaf depth $C$ has at most $2^C$ leaves. This remains true when legal transcript lengths vary: the leaves are prefix-free. Summing their subnormalized contributions gives

$$
F_s=\sum_t a_{t\mid s}\le 2^Cq.
$$

Averaging over the public seed gives the theorem. A zero-probability leaf contributes zero and causes no normalization issue. If $C=0$, there is only the empty transcript for each seed, recovering the no-message bound.

For a target $F\ge f>0$, an equivalent necessary condition is

$$
C\ge \lambda m-\log_2(1/f).
$$

The right-hand side can be negative; the independent constraint $C\ge0$ still applies.

### The shallow-depth exponential form

If $L\ge64(rD+1)$, then

$$
m\ge\frac{N}{8192(rD+1)^2}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
F\le
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
2^{\,C-aN/(rD+1)^2}
\right\},
\qquad
a=\frac{\log_2(17/16)}{8192}.
$$

The constant is explicit and conservative. It is not optimized. In particular, if $D$ is fixed and $C=o(N)$, the unconditional ground-space weight is exponentially small in $N$.

### A uniform statement including the local-preparation endpoint

Let $\gamma=\log_2(1/f)$. From $C+\gamma\ge\lambda m$,

$$
\lambda(m+1)
\le C+\gamma+\lambda
\le \max\{1,\gamma+\lambda\}(C+1).
$$

For every $L,D$,

$$
m+1\ge
\frac{N}{8192(rD+1)^2}.
$$

To check the last inequality, put $x=L/[32(rD+1)]$. If $x\ge1$, then $\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2$; if $x<1$, then $m+1=1>x^2/4$. Since $rD+1\le r(D+1)$ for $r\ge1$, we obtain the all-size bound

$$
\boxed{
(C+1)(D+1)^2
\ge
\frac{\lambda N}
{8192r^2\max\{1,\log_2(1/f)+\lambda\}} .
}
$$

This formulation allows $C=0$ when $D$ is of order $L$. It does not extend a strictly decaying exponential estimate into a regime where exact local preparation is possible.

## 4. Expected communication and prefix coding

Let the protocol terminate almost surely, with countably many finite leaf transcripts, and retain the same branchwise depth cap $D$. Its mean communication is

$$
\overline C=\mathbb E\,\ell(\mathsf T).
$$

**Theorem.** For every such protocol,

$$
\boxed{
\overline C
\ge H(\mathsf T\mid\mathsf S)
\ge
\max\!\left\{0,\,
F\log_2\frac{F}{q_{L,r}(D)}
\right\}.
}
$$

We use $0\log_2(0/q)=0$. Conditional entropy here is the average discrete Shannon entropy of the transcript distribution for a fixed seed; it is not differential entropy, even if the seed is continuously distributed.

### Ground-space weight forces transcript entropy

Fix a seed $s$ for which termination is almost sure; joint almost sure termination guarantees this for almost every seed. All seedwise probability distributions below are taken on this full-measure set. Put

$$
p_t=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{t\mid s},
\qquad
a_t=\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{TC}}\sigma_{t\mid s}),
\qquad
F_s=\sum_t a_t.
$$

We have $0\le a_t\le p_t$, $\sum_t p_t=1$, and $a_t\le q$. Introduce a mathematical binary random variable $G$ with joint probabilities

$$
\Pr(\mathsf T=t,G=1\mid s)=a_t,\qquad
\Pr(\mathsf T=t,G=0\mid s)=p_t-a_t.
$$

This is the distribution obtained by imagining a final measurement of $P_{\mathrm{TC}}$. No such measurement or success signal is used by the preparation protocol.

When $F_s>0$,

$$
\Pr(\mathsf T=t\mid G=1,s)
=\frac{a_t}{F_s}
\le \frac{q}{F_s}.
$$

Therefore the entropy of this conditional distribution is at least $\log_2(F_s/q)$. Entropy concavity, equivalently conditioning on a binary random variable, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(\mathsf T\mid s)
&\ge H(\mathsf T\mid G,s)\\
&\ge F_s H(\mathsf T\mid G=1,s)\\
&\ge F_s\log_2(F_s/q).
\end{aligned}
$$

For $F_s=0$ the right-hand side is defined as zero, and the inequality follows from entropy nonnegativity. For $F_s=1$ the absent $G=0$ term is simply omitted. The calculation remains valid with infinite entropies, using nonnegative countable sums.

The function $x\mapsto x\log_2(x/q)$ is convex on $[0,1]$ with its continuous value at zero. Averaging over $\mathsf S$ and using $\mathbb E F_{\mathsf S}=F$ yields

$$
H(\mathsf T\mid\mathsf S)
\ge F\log_2(F/q).
$$

Entropy is also nonnegative, which gives the displayed positive part.

### Physical transcript length bounds entropy

For each seed, the codeword lengths of the leaf transcripts satisfy Kraft's inequality:

$$
\sum_t2^{-\ell(t)}\le1.
$$

For almost every seed the leaf probabilities sum to one, and the source-coding inequality consequently gives

$$
\mathbb E[\ell(\mathsf T)\mid s]\ge H(\mathsf T\mid s).
$$

One direct proof normalizes $2^{-\ell(t)}$ to a distribution and applies nonnegativity of classical relative entropy. For a countably infinite leaf set, truncate the binary tree at depth $k$, treating unfinished prefixes as temporary leaves. The finite-tree inequality holds for every $k$; monotone refinement and almost sure termination yield the stated limit. If the expected length is infinite, the lower bound is automatic. Averaging over the seed completes the theorem.

The protocol tree, rather than an observer's compressed description of an execution, determines $\ell(t)$. An encoder may reduce physical communication by using a genuine variable-length code, but its realized codewords must satisfy the same prefix rule.

### Uniform expected-cost frontier

For $F\ge f>0$, write $\kappa=1/(e\ln2)$. Since $x\log_2x\ge-\kappa$ on $[0,1]$,

$$
\overline C\ge F\lambda m+F\log_2F
\ge f\lambda m-\kappa.
$$

The numerical constants obey $\kappa+f\lambda<1$ for $0<f\le1$. Thus

$$
\overline C+1\ge f\lambda(m+1),
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{
(\overline C+1)(D+1)^2
\ge\frac{f\lambda N}{8192r^2}.
}
$$

Rare expensive branches therefore do not remove the area-per-causal-patch cost at fixed nonzero unconditional fidelity.

## 5. Selected success events

Let $\sigma^{\mathrm{acc}}_{t\mid s}\le\sigma_{t\mid s}$ be the subnormalized accepted contribution selected by a classical predicate on existing branches and private classical outcomes. The operator inequality follows by retaining a subset of the positive branch contributions. The fixed-transcript bound also gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}
(P_{\mathrm{TC}}\sigma^{\mathrm{acc}}_{t\mid s})
\le q.
$$

With a hard communication cap, summing proves

$$
p f_{\mathrm{acc}}\le2^Cq,
$$

where $p$ is the acceptance probability and $f_{\mathrm{acc}}$ the conditional ground-space weight. This remains a valid converse even when an external classical predicate of the completed records is evaluated for free. It does not grant an additional nonlocal quantum filtering operation.

It is not a matching operational heralding theorem. Making a distributed acceptance condition available at a designated site can require additional communication or local depth. Those costs must be included when a physical success flag is part of the task.

## 6. Relation to the construction and prior work

The [block preparation](preparation.md) uses local depth $O(\ell)$ and at most $2(B-1)$ point-to-point delivered bits, with $B=\lfloor L/\ell\rfloor^2$, and produces the exact specified ground state. Together with the bounds above, it gives the optimal scaling of both hard and mean communication at fixed fidelity, including the wholly local endpoint.

The underlying geometric-entanglement exponent and anyon obstruction are inherited from [Li–Lee–Yoshida](../references/related-work.md#lly25-spatial-geometric-entanglement). The contribution here is their operational conversion into a physically charged classical-information frontier, with a local-channel proof that does not factor the target from arbitrary discarded environments. [Related work](../references/related-work.md) distinguishes this from qualitative local-feedback impossibility results, measurement-region bounds, and free-feedforward constructions.
